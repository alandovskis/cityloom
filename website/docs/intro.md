# Introduction

CityLoom is a street, junction and city-map editor. It is written in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly and drawn with
[Leptos](https://leptos.dev/) 0.8 (client-side rendering). It runs in the browser, which keeps your work. A server
(`cityloom_server`) serves its files and offers optional sign-in; a person who chooses to can store their work there
and receive it on each device they sign in on.

The streets come from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/). The rules, rates and thresholds of the
catalogue (what a check measures a street against) are synthetic placeholders, not a standard.

The app is available in English and Canadian French.

| Street   | `web/street.html`       | Edit one street, drawn in 2.5D: arrange its pieces, read the checks. The Impact tab of the Notes panel opens with a Last change card: after each edit, undo, redo or start over (a grouped operation such as Make pedestrian is one change) it lists the width each use gained or lost, then the checks that now pass and the ones that now fail, and says whether the checks improved, regressed or both; it is a polite live region, updated a moment after the edits settle. The width table is under it. The Transform menu also offers five street templates (Commercial main street, School street, Neighbourhood greenway, Bus corridor, Industrial access street): each lays ordinary pieces out in the selected zone, fitted to the street's width, as one change you can undo and keep editing; one that does not suit the street (a freeway, or a width outside what the template can use) stays listed, disabled, with its reason. The School street also makes a school zone, as far as each part fits: a stretch of the street (or the selected zone as a whole when it is too short to set one apart) with a speed limit of 30 km/h and a raised table at its middle; it says which parts it applied and which it skipped, and why, and one Undo takes it all back. A zone has a speed limit of its own, set from the Street tab, or follows the street's. Along the street you can put curbside uses, transit stops, trees and planting (tree pits, beds, rain gardens, bioswales). A Raised tables, crossings, chicanes and modal filters row under the stops lets you add, move, resize and remove raised tables (speed tables), raised crossings, chicanes (build-outs from alternate curbs that make the road bend; you pick the side the first one is on) and modal filters (a barrier across the roadway that stops motor traffic and leaves a way through for people on foot and on bikes) along the street, with refusals that say why and checks for their length, the speed limit (lower for a chicane), bus and bike lanes (a chicane cannot cross one; a modal filter cannot cross a bus lane and needs a sidewalk or a bike lane beside it), transit stops and junctions; the 2.5D draws tables with their ramps, chicanes as blocks reaching into the roadway and a modal filter as one block across the motor lanes. |

The street and junction pages are opened from the map: the map gives each place an address such as `?street=7` or
`?junction=3`, with the project it belongs to (`&project=<id>`). A page opened that way edits one place of the city in
that project, and its edits are kept in the browser's storage, apart from every other project's.
