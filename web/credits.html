CityLoom is built on other people's work. This page says whose, and what is still a stand-in.

Transit measures

The transit priority measures in the editors, and their names and codes, follow the toolbox of the Transit Priority Atlas, published by the Marron Institute. CityLoom draws them in its own way and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Atlas or the Marron Institute.

Streets

The streets, their lanes and the junctions come from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database Licence. Places are found with Nominatim and the roads are fetched with the Overpass API, both public services of the OpenStreetMap community. osm2streets (Apache License 2.0) turns the map data into streets and junctions. Where CityLoom is deployed with tiles of the Montréal area, those are cut from a Geofabrik extract of the same data.

The map

The basemap behind the city map is drawn from OpenStreetMap data, © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), in the OpenMapTiles schema (© OpenMapTiles, CC BY 4.0), made with Planetiler (Apache License 2.0) from a Geofabrik extract, with Natural Earth (public domain) and OpenStreetMap water polygons at low zooms. Where CityLoom is deployed with the basemap's tiles, those are built from these sources. It is drawn with MapLibre GL JS and read with PMTiles, both under the BSD 3-Clause license: read the MapLibre license, read the PMTiles license. Its labels are set in Noto Sans, copyright The Noto Project Authors, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1: read the license.

Typeface

The pages are set in the system's own typeface. The CityLoom logotype is drawn from Overpass, copyright 2021 The Overpass Project Authors, used under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Read the license.

Software

  • Rust compiles the editing models and the pages to WebAssembly.
  • Leptos draws the pages in the browser.
  • wasm-bindgen 0.2.129 joins the Rust code to the page.
  • serde 1.0.229 and serde_json 1.0.151 carry the drawing from Rust to the page.

Numbers

Every rule, rate and threshold the checks use is a synthetic placeholder, not a measurement or a standard. The widths of the streets are what OpenStreetMap says of their lanes, or a typical lane where it says nothing.
